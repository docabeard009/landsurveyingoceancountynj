How much does a land survey cost in NJ?

There's no flat rate, and any surveyor who quotes one without the address is guessing. Here's what actually sets the price — so you can tell a fair quote from a bad one.

Request a quote 917.463.6042
The short answer

A residential land survey in New Jersey is priced by the job, not by a flat rate. The main drivers are the type of survey (a corner stakeout costs less than a title survey, which costs less than a topographic survey), lot size and shape, how good the deed and prior survey records are, whether corner markers can be recovered or must be re-established, terrain and vegetation, and turnaround time. A licensed surveyor can give a firm quote from the address, usually the same day.

What determines the price

  • Survey type. From least to most involved: corner marking or stakeout; boundary survey; title survey for a closing; elevation certificate; topographic survey with CAD; ALTA/NSPS survey. Each adds field time, research or deliverables.
  • Lot size and shape. A 60-by-100 shore lot is a very different job than a two-acre wooded parcel in Colts Neck or a lagoon lot with an irregular waterfront line.
  • Record quality. A clean deed with a recent survey and recoverable pins is quick. A deed that references a 1940s map with monuments that no longer exist means research and retracement across neighboring lots.
  • Monument recovery. If your corners are in the ground, we find them. If they're gone, re-establishing them from adjoining properties is the biggest variable in residential pricing.
  • Terrain and access. Heavy brush, steep grades, tidal areas and locked yards add field time.
  • Deliverables. A sealed plat is standard; CAD files, extra certified copies and FEMA forms add scope.
  • Turnaround. Rush closings and permit deadlines can be accommodated; expedited scheduling is priced accordingly.

Why survey quotes vary so much

Two homeowners on the same street can get very different prices — one has a 2019 survey with pins in the ground, the other has a 1962 deed and a yard that's been re-landscaped three times. The lower quote isn't always the better one, either. A survey that skips monument recovery and just plots the deed on paper is cheap and, for a fence or a dispute, nearly useless. Ask what the quote includes: research, field recovery of corners, setting markers, and a sealed plat should all be in it.

Why we don't publish a price list

Because it would be wrong for your lot. What we do instead: send us the address and what you need it for — closing, fence, pool, addition, dispute, flood insurance — and we'll look up the record, check the lot, and give you a firm number, usually the same day. The survey cost calculator gives a ballpark range by survey type while you wait.

Ways to keep the cost down

  • Dig out any prior survey from your closing papers — it can cut research time significantly.
  • If you need a survey and an elevation certificate, order them together; one site visit is cheaper than two.
  • Give us the deadline early. Rush work costs more than scheduled work.
  • Don't defer it. A survey before a fence or pool is a fraction of the cost of fixing an encroachment after.

Questions

Survey cost FAQ

How much does a boundary survey cost in NJ?+

It depends on lot size, record quality and whether corners can be recovered. Small lots with good records are at the low end; large, wooded or poorly documented parcels cost more. Send the address for a firm quote.

How much does an elevation certificate cost?+

Less than a full survey in most cases, since it documents elevations rather than establishing boundaries. Proximity to a benchmark and the structure's complexity drive the price. Combining it with a survey on one visit saves money.

How much does a topographic survey cost?+

More than a boundary survey — it adds elevation work across the lot and a CAD deliverable. Lot size and how much needs to be located (trees, structures, utilities) set the price.

How much does it cost to have property corners marked?+

Usually the least expensive service, if a prior survey exists and the corners can be recovered. If they have to be re-established from scratch, it becomes a boundary survey.

Why are land surveys expensive?+

You're paying for a licensed professional's research into the deed history, field work with survey-grade equipment, recovery or re-establishment of legal monuments, and a sealed document that carries liability. It's the only thing that legally establishes where your property is.

Do you give free estimates?+

Yes. Send us the address and what the survey is for, and we'll quote it at no charge, usually the same day.

Is the cheapest surveyor a bad idea?+

Not necessarily, but ask what's included. A quote that skips field recovery of corners and just plots the deed is cheap because it's not doing the work that makes a survey useful.

Does the survey cost more for a rush closing?+

Expedited scheduling is available and priced accordingly. Tell us the closing date up front; if there's room, we'll fit it in at the standard rate.
